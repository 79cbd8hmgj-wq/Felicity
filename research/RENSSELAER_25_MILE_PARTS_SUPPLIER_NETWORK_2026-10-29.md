## Research method for every new upgrade going forward

1. **Discovery independent from the existing 255-ID catalog:** manufacturer first-party specs + applicable Toyota service material + reputable aftermarket/interchange + real owner evidence. Avoid the previous trap of only finding things inside existing Felicity records.
2. **Local supplier landscape:** for plausible parts, cross-search **AutoZone, Advance, NAPA, O'Reilly, Carquest, Fisher, Auto Value/Riverside, Universal, import specialists, two Toyota parts desks and used-OEM yards**; include Harbor Freight when tools materially affect DIY feasibility. Check each individual store for pickup/in-stock/special-order versus not orderable. Include 25-mile center and only list wider outliers if explicitly labeled.
3. **Full real job cost:** exact hardware brand/part number, actual local in-stock pickup price, alternative part-line pricing, clips/fittings/fluids, tool rental or purchase, tax and car-specific fitment gate. A store listing for `2004 Corolla` is not proof of June-2003 TMMC compatibility.
4. **Engineering value:** classify actual improvement against a **healthy factory car**, known original defect correction, new added function, or just restore/maintenance. Ask whether a $10 preventative part is a credible safer improvement than a $200 system adding leaks.
5. **Safety / vehicle condition:** current front suspension banging and underbody rust must be inspected before driving performance trials and before adding mounts, heavy loading or lower-profile wheels. No SRS/ABS hydraulic retrofits as casual DIY.
6. **Provenance:** store page and manufacturer documentation separately; catalog stock ≠ real-time inventory; manufacturers' marketing claims ≠ measured Corolla improvement; different physical functions get new UPG IDs, extra brands do not.
