# Felicity — Brake-system modernization evidence gate (2026-10-25)

Scope: owner-described US 2004 Corolla LE, June 2003 Canadian build, 1ZZ-FE, automatic. **Research, not a completed repair or a verified per-VIN parts order.** Focus: wet grip, road-salt corrosion, quietness, reliable pedal feel and genuinely incremental improvements over a healthy factory braking system.

## Brake architecture and fitment control

- OE catalog diagrams show **front disc / rear drum** applications for 2004 Corolla, but broader catalogs also expose other configurations and rear disc options. Establish **actual installed front caliper, rear drum/disc, ABS equipment and June-build VIN fitment** before specifying components. Some online Toyota catalog listings return multiple rotor/drum variations despite generic trim labels.
- Tire Rack's specific **2004 Corolla LE** catalog lists **Akebono ProACT ACT923A front ceramic pad axle set** (observed $61 in retrieved listing), and makes claims concerning noise, dust and rotor friendliness. This is a **vendor-supported application lead**, not proof that it exceeds new factory pads in stopping distance, cold/wet friction, or life.

## No-purchase baseline inspection

1. Record brake warning lamps/ABS, firm consistent pedal, straight deceleration, parking brake retention and any pull/pulse/noise. If pedal sinks, brake lines leak, ABS warning indicates a fault, or lines are severely corroded, **do not perform experimental road tests**: repair first.
2. With appropriately supported vehicle (never jack alone), inspect hard lines where routing traps winter salt, hose cracking/abrasion and flexible-to-hard-line joints; note dampness. Include master cylinder reservoir/booster, lines near fuel tank/rear axle, caliper sliders/boots, rear drum cylinders and shoes.
3. Measure pad thickness, rotor thickness/runout **against build-specific factory service limits**; inspect drum lining diameter and adjusters. No invented limit numbers in this document. Check rotor friction-side corrosion, heat spots and pad contact pattern.
4. Document actual caliper casting and rear brake type; check ABS sensor/tone equipment and parking brake design before accepting catalog fitment.
5. If service needed: use correct Toyota-specified brake-fluid grade, properly sourced friction/hardware and torque/bleeding sequence from early-build Toyota repair manual. Keep clean grease only at specified non-friction contact points; never grease rotors or pads.
6. Establish tire age/condition and inflation before evaluating road braking. Braking distances depend strongly on tire grip, weather and surface; **do not perform maximum-effort public-road stopping trials**.

## Verification and evidence standard

A manufacturer’s coating, friction or low-dust description is not a demonstrated comparative benefit versus healthy factory equipment. To approve a brake modernization over OEM, seek independent matched same-car tests of wet/cold stopping, fade, dust, rotor wear, NVH and pedal feel. Existing catalog applications must be refined to **VIN/build, equipment and dimensions**. No installed hardware and no measured benefit asserted.

**Disposition:** continue prioritizing tire/wheel research and brake condition inspection. Investigate Akebono ACT923A and nonfriction-area coated rotors as next-service choices, not immediate unnecessary replacements. Hold hose, hydraulic, disc conversion and rotor drilling schemes.
