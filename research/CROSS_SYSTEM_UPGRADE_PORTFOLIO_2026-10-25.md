# Felicity — Cross-system modernization portfolio and sequencing (2026-10-25)

**Purpose:** Combine existing approved-for-research upgrade work into a realistic road-trip/daily-use program. This is an *evidence-gated decision portfolio*, not a parts order, claims of measured gains, or an installed-work record.

**Subject vehicle:** owner-identified US 2004 Toyota Corolla LE, Canadian June-2003 build, 1ZZ-FE/A245E, aftermarket intake and 18-inch wheels. Physical equipment and June-build part compatibility are still verification gates. OM12859U covers October-2003 onward builds, so its specifications are model-family reference only.

## 1. Decision rules

- Compare (a) **healthy unmodified factory Corolla** and (b) **this owner's presently modified Corolla** separately. Returning current aftermarket parts to OEM can improve (b) without exceeding (a).
- A maintenance repair that restores as-designed operation is vital but **not counted as healthy-OEM modernization**.
- Account for total installed cost: parts, shipping/tax, installation consumables, tools, professional work where needed, inspection and reversibility. No item has a guaranteed MPG/dBA/ride/stopping improvement for this car.
- Avoid adding overlapping benefits: wheel/tire package and braking tire-grip benefit are **one project**; two types of acoustic treatment have different mechanisms and should be localized by test; cooler and higher-capacity radiator are not justified merely by being listed together.
- Tier 0: safety and current-condition repair gates; Tier 1: low-risk reversible controlled trials; Tier 2: capital-cost modifications only on corroborated need. Do not score absent measurements as zero cost or proven improvements.

## 3. What should happen together versus separately

**Baseline before upgrades:**
1. Check oil level (history included a potentially near-empty event), leaks, brakes/hard lines, structural corrosion, tire age and wear, battery, cooling leaks and open VIN recalls. Repair any safety hazards before road tests.
2. Capture current hardware: both intake and MAF housing, four tire/wheel specifications, exact brake configuration, existing cabin filter and cruise option, and fluid/service history. Do not publish identifiable VIN.
3. Collect at least three comparable route loops with tire pressure within applicable guidelines, ambient weather, passenger/fuel loading, measured speed, surface type, subjective 1–5 impact harshness and road-noise notes. A phone sound app is a *relative* repeat instrument, not a certified dBA measurement.

**Stage A: cheap/reversible refinement:** baseline and carbon-filter check; removable solar shade when justified; acoustics only after locating a transmission path. These can be tested individually, not layered all at once.

**Stage B: tire/wheel capital decision:** make a dimensionally verified 15-inch tire/wheel package a **single** capital project; inspect steering/suspension before blaming wheels alone. Compare all-weather/winter requirements for Rensselaer County. Tire Rack's Tour54 is a *candidate*, not a proof of Corolla-specific benefits. Do not infer actual gas mileage/speedometer bias solely from nominal diameter.

**Stage C: deeper hardware only if symptoms demand:** accessory-drive decoupler if identified as a meaningful NVH source with confirmed installed compatibility; OE intake completion if A/B tests document an improvement against this car; hydraulic/brake upgrades only with verified need. No cooler merely because the car is old.

| Acoustic absorber reduces cabin road noise | 3M material spec / absorption mechanism | sound path and controlled in-car trials | Trapped moisture, trim interference, wasted expense |
| Healthy drivetrain benefits from cooler | Thermal engineering mechanism | real A245E ATF temp, thermal profile, cold climate | extra hoses/leaks, impaired warm-up |
| Higher-output alternator saves fuel | None | matched parasitic demand test under same electrical load | cost and possibly extra load/weight |
