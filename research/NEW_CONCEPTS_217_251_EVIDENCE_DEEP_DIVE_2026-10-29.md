# Felicity — evidence verification of all 35 newly discovered upgrade concepts UPG-217–251

**Research review:** October 29, 2026  
**Individual car:** U.S.-market 2004 Toyota Corolla LE, **June 2003 TMMC Canadian production**, `ZZE130L-DEPNKA`, 1ZZ-FE / A245E automatic, with at least one observed non-OE **215/45R18** tire. Current loud bilateral front suspension bangs remain **safety uninspected**, so no driving comparison, performance road testing or suspension installation approval is authorized.

**Mission:** Reassess the 35 discoveries made after original `UPG-216`, instead of adding more speculative concepts. Trace OEM/manufacturer fitment, new functionality compared with healthy stock, economics, installation and durability risks. No vehicle inspection has occurred; **no new hardware is `confirmed`, `vehicle-validated` or `purchase_ready=yes`**. This report separates documentary facts from engineering deductions. Each of the 35 `upgrade_research.csv` and `upgrade_value_matrix.csv` records received an updated investigation gate, review code and decision. Sixteen got significant new factual evidence or correction and seven *unique* references (not previously registered) were added to `sources.csv`; existing URLs were reused, not duplicated.

## Bottom line: which candidates actually deserve effort?

The useful result of this pass is not 35 purchase recommendations. **Several are credible, purpose-dependent improvements; some are costly/technically hostile; a few can make a healthy Corolla worse.** Prioritize the mechanical safety inspection before using any handling candidate as a remedy for current banging. The following order is a practical *research queue*, not a numerical vehicle score.

## Required on-car evidence with minimal work

Before even considering purchase quotes or removing important suspension hardware, collect:

1. **Safety before road-testing:** independent inspection of both front struts/mounts, lower arms/ball joints, sway-bar links, brakes/caliper hardware, subframe/fasteners and wheel torque. Severe bangs must not be hidden by adjustable shocks, stiff bushes, bumper kits or other accessories.
2. **Four-wheel/tire baseline:** photograph all tire sidewalls, driver's door tire placard and wheel bead/hub mounting. Verify all four installed assemblies, clearance and proper winter-size 15in wheels before UPG-221.
3. **Factory option inventory:** under both front seats (rear heater duct vents, UPG-246); washer tank/connector and cluster icon (UPG-247); rear bumper fog openings and harness (UPG-220); factory ABS warning/actuator (UPG-234); seat side airbags/OCS (UPG-218).
4. **Salt condition survey:** underside rear frame rails/rocker pinch welds, rear axle mounts, suspension mount points, brake pipes/lines, door drain holes. Resolve structural rot before UPG-217/242/227.
5. **Electrical design:** with alternator under normal load and cold/warm conditions, measure **maximum** 12V bus voltage; otherwise UPG-243 maker voltage limit cannot be resolved. Only after chosen accessories, plan UPG-228 fused distribution/alternator capacity. No modifications to SRS or ABS harnesses.
6. **Purpose check:** Do you actually use a bike rack, rear passengers in deep cold, cramped parking, dusty-air environments, or recurring washer-fluid loss? The presence of a catalogued product does not itself justify a vehicle modification.

## Bottom-line decision discipline

- **Likely highest practical real-life value after safety check:** 195/65R15 four-wheel winter traction package (`221`), suitably applied salt protection of actually sound chassis and internal cavities (`242/217`), genuine rear-seat HVAC ducts *if absent* (`246`), floorliners *if a securely fitting SKU is available* (`230`).
- **Documented installation-ready in model-year scope but not on actual vehicle:** `223` 2003–08 Corolla hood struts and `227` 2004 Corolla hitch are convincing manufacturer application leads, conditional on no rust and suitable work.
- **Real feature but hard integration:** Canadian washer warning (`247`), seat heaters (`218`), alert sensors (`219/239`), aftermarket electronics (`226/228/229`) and handling-altering rear beam polyurethane (`249`).
- **Avoid marketing-as-engineering:** especially `251` 'new bump stop' vs healthy OE, `245` costly cold-sensitive reserve lithium, `243` heated wiper overvoltage, `232` external oil plumbing, `234` complete ABS retrofit.
- **No cost claim** beyond anchored parts-only prices. A full DIY/pro job may include freight, tools, corrosion work, tire mounting, alignment, calibration, professional labor and extra brackets. Empty original price fields remain unknown, not zero. New per-model data comes from first-party makers, original Toyota document reproductions and catalogues, not vehicle measurements.
