## 2. UPG-242 — exposed-undercarriage lanolin film, not hidden cavity wax

**Manufacturer:** FLUID FILM wet/non-drying lanolin-based corrosion inhibitor, aerosol and NAS bulk. Primary manufacturer resources:
- https://www.fluid-film.com/automotive-applications/
- https://www.fluid-film.com/frequently-asked-questions/
- Technical material PDF: https://fluidfilm.no/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Teknisk_datablad_usa_NAS-2.pdf

The maker describes a flexible barrier that does not become brittle like some hard undercoatings, and can wet the metal surface; sources also explicitly warn that it can **swell non-oil-resistant rubber**, affect some existing paint-type undercoatings and remain tacky/dirty. This is not a proven lifetime or measured rust-rate benefit on the actual Corolla and does **not** turn structurally unsound rust into roadworthy metal.

**Why it matters:** New York brine/salt exposure is a relevant real-world stressor. A healthy, structurally sound original metal surface can benefit from additional external protection without a Toyota defect being identified. The program's existing *internal* cavity wax study addresses the inaccessible inside of rockers and doors; this class is about exposed underbody arms, bolts and appropriate metal structure.

**Safe feasibility:** Assess frame/suspension/rear brake lines for rot before coating. Clean/dry properly, never conceal advanced corrosion, and keep all chemical off brake rotors, pads, drums, tires, belts and exhaust/catalyst. Respect labels on rubber boots, factory seam sealer and connectors. For a paved driveway DIY application, use manufacturer-approved mechanical vehicle supports and appropriate ventilation/PPE. Repeat inspection/reapply as exposure and wear warrant. No USD cost quote without real area/material needs and job method.

## 3. UPG-243 — electrically heated wiper elements (and voltage conflict)

**Manufacturer:** Everblades 12 V heated windshield wiper kit, listed **USD 150** for **two electrically heated blade/frame assemblies, fused wiring harness and cabin switch**. Primary product https://www.everblades.com/heated-windshield-wiper-kit/ and detailed maker installation https://www.everblades.com/installation/ .

**Benefit above a healthy original:** active heating of blade rubber and pivots during sleet/snow is a function no healthy conventional Corolla wiper has. It is not simply another winter-branded or silicone blade.

**Critical gate requiring attention:** Everblades explicitly warns *do not connect to over 14 volts DC* and says the blade frame heating wire can reach very high temperatures (user burn risk). **The Corolla charging regulator may produce voltage above 14 V during some operating conditions**, so the installation cannot be endorsed until that normal output is logged and Everblades gives a compatible design/permission. Never improvise unsafe voltage regulation or tap the stock DRL/headlamp circuit. Use electrically protected ignition-run supply only after checking manufacturer fusing/wiring, correct 2004 Corolla blade arm hook and sweep lengths, wiper full travel and routed wire flex. Heated blades are not a windshield defroster replacement; retain the dashboard defrost controls.

**Performance evidence:** Maker claim only; no independent exact Corolla snow accumulation or stopping/sightline benefit measured. Current body/wiper weather seal condition is unknown.

## 4. UPG-244 — stitched 360-degree surround vision, not merely a camera count

**Manufacturer:** Metra **iBEAM `TE-360-3D`** universal four-camera 3D Surround View Monitor system with DVR and included calibration equipment, installed with front/rear/left/right wide-angle lenses. Current maker product: https://www.metraonline.com/product/TE-360-3D . Current manufacturer 360 View category lists **USD 2,071.99 MSRP** as viewed 2026-10-29: https://www.metraonline.com/brand/ibeam/i-allviews . Previously indexed development-page MSRP **USD 1,496** conflicts with the current manufacturer category; do not treat either as guaranteed current street price.

**Function:** Dedicated vision processor stitches all four camera inputs into an overhead car-and-surroundings rendering. That supports curb proximity and low-speed maneuvering with a wider visual perspective than UPG-135 backup camera, not autonomous detection or collision avoidance. The virtual view can have blind spots, perspective distortion and seam-calibration errors; use mirrors and direct vision regardless.

**High-friction installation:** Four sealed external mounting positions, 12 V wiring, display/output interface, vehicle body penetrations, weather durability, full calibration mat space and front/rear image distortion correction. Consumer retrofit would be expensive relative to Corolla value, and professional fitment is required. Current manufacturer 24-ft max vehicle size does not certify 2004 TMMC mounting. Avoid equating a single EchoMaster `PCAM-360` camera with a stitched four-channel system.

## 5. UPG-245 — lithium starter with reserve energy and RE-START, poor practical default

**Manufacturer:** Antigravity Batteries **`AG-35-40-RS`** Group 35/Q85 LiFePO4 automotive battery, first-party `https://antigravitybatteries.com/products/starter-batteries/automotive/ag-35-rs/`. Maker lists **USD 949.99**, Group 35 case **9 × 6.85 × 8.6 in**, **40 Ah**, **1500 cranking amps**, BMS, **M8 terminal connection**, and keyfob/manual *RE-START* after internal low-charge isolation. This adds a new capability beyond traditional AGM performance or a nonessential 12V accessory disconnect.

**Major technical caveat:** Actual original Corolla battery group/terminal orientation, charging voltage and thermal support must be confirmed. The battery manufacturer's *automotive RE-START* manual warns that at or below about **39°F** initial crank may be sluggish because of lithium resistance, and users may need battery self-warming: https://antigravitybatteries.com/downloads/2019-AG-Automotive-RS-Batteries-User-Manual.pdf . This is a legitimate concern for Albany-area cold starts. The actual 2004 Toyota alternator/charging system and lithium BMS cold-charge cutoff, engine-bay temperature and reserve longevity must be authenticated by the supplier before any installation.

**Decision:** Legitimate technology discovery, but **poor value** for this project's cost/safety/road-trip objectives compared to a proper conventional Group 35 lead-acid or AGM battery and separate externally charged jump starter. Does not support an MPG or durability gain, and available data do not justify buying.

## 6. UPG-246 — Corolla OE All-Weather Guard rear footwell heat duct architecture

**Factory source:** Toyota 2004 US Corolla original manufacturer brochure (PDF p.3 / zero-based screenshot page 2), *AVAILABLE PACKAGES AND OPTIONS*: **LE All-Weather Guard Package** lists windshield molding, a heavy-duty heater and **rear-seat heater ducts**. https://www.auto-brochures.com/makes/Toyota/Corolla/Toyota_US%20Corolla_2004.pdf . Thus the function was a legitimate factory optional package, not a conjectured later-generation design.

**Genuine NAP Corolla parts reference:** https://www.toyotapartsdeal.com/parts-list/2004-toyota-corolla-nap_sedan_hatchback/electrical/heating_air_conditioning_control_air_duct.html lists **87212-02070 (rear air duct No.1 / RH)**, **87213-02070 (No.2 / LH)**, **87215-02050 (rear duct No.3)**, **87216-02040 (No.4)** for the NAP Corolla branches including ZZE130 4FC. Dealer table with product positions https://www.toyotapartsnow.com/parts-list/2004-toyota-corolla/body-hardware/ducts presents individual part prices of roughly **USD 81.59 ×2** for the primary ducts plus **USD 33/55** extensions, excluding clips, air-box source parts, trim work and tax. No complete kit is proven.

**Potential healthy-system improvement:** A non-All-Weather-Guard original with missing rear ductwork could gain a real OEM-designed under-front-seat heater path, potentially improving rear footwell warmth without overdriving blower motor or replacing healthy front HVAC. This matters for rear passengers in New York winters and balances comfort across the car.

**Important uncertainty:** The exact vehicle may already have the option, and catalog parts presence does **not** prove ducts are absent or all listed parts are needed. Inspect rear-seat vents/under-seat outlet openings, heater control/duct source, carpet moldings and package sticker, then request private VIN-specific Toyota EPC. Some Canadian/2005/E120 listings use **different** `87212-12120` / `87213-12080`; do not mix Japanese production codes with Canadian NAP `ZZE130L-DEPNKA`. If absent, produce full BOM including fasteners, seals and any upstream ducts; measure rear outlet temperature/air volume rather than promise a percentage comfort gain. DIY intermediate only after trim/SRS clearance validated.
