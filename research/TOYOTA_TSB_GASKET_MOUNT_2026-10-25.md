# Felicity — Factory TSB evidence: revised intake gasket and right engine mount (2026-10-25)

**Scope**: 2004 US Corolla LE, Canadian June 2003 production, owner-identified 1ZZ-FE / automatic. TSB citations are original Toyota bulletin reproductions. **TSB applicability and actual vehicle diagnosis are separate things**. This document adds evidence to the research branch, not an installed repair or a validated purchase.

## Strong factory-supported lead: EG045-07, intake manifold gasket

**Source**: Toyota bulletin EG045-07 dated October 27, 2007; original five-page PDF: https://static.oemdtc.com/TSB/EG045-07.pdf (p.1 applicability/parts, p.2 test, pp.3-5 repair).

**Applicability:** 2003–2008 Corolla and Matrix 1ZZ-FE; includes this vehicle's *model year and engine*, even though it precedes the October 2003-onward owner-manual scope.

**Failure mechanism:** vacuum leak at intake-manifold/head interface after subfreezing overnight cold soak. Potential engine light with P0171, P0300, P0301–P0304, P0133. The bulletin stresses test **after cold soak** because condition may disappear warm.

**Toyota-listed replacement:** previous **17171-22030** and **17171-0D020** → current **17171-22060**, intake-manifold-to-cylinder-head gasket No.1 (one). Manufacturer documentation supports this as an updated repair component for the stated condition—not proof of superior power, MPG or service life in an already perfectly sealing engine.

**Diagnostic gate:** capture DTCs and short-term fuel trim on a cold engine per factory diagnostic equipment. Verify the leak location using a safe professional technique; Toyota's procedure says if fuel trim does not confirm the targeted leak, diagnose other vacuum leaks or lean causes instead. Avoid casually applying flammable spray at a running hot engine and avoid replacing oxygen sensors based on P0133 alone.

**Service complexity:** bulletin repair includes coolant drain, disconnection of evaporative-vapor hoses/connectors, manifold removal, surface cleaning, replacement gasket and coolant refill. This is **intermediate DIY**, not a trivial five-minute gasket swap; follow the original 2003/2004 factory procedure and appropriate safety practice. No blanket preemptive replacement recommendation.

**Priority:** P1 *condition-triggered*, elevated to P0 diagnosis if significant misfire/driveability fault.

## Separate TSB: EG027-06, improved RH engine mount

**Source:** Toyota bulletin EG027-06 dated April 21, 2006; https://static.oemdtc.com/TSB/EG027-06.pdf (p.1 application and parts, p.2 repair).

**What Toyota actually documents:** 2005–2006 model Corolla and Matrix 1ZZ-FE, *before listed specific production change VINs*, may experience vibration/drone from takeoff to 30 mph. Right engine mount improved: **12305-0D022 → 12305-0D120**. Toyota expressly states improvement for that symptom on applicable cars.

**Critical applicability limit:** **2004 is excluded from the published TSB's stated model-year scope.** The earlier Felicity mount-catalog study identified 2002–Jan-2004 **12305-0D021**, Jan-2004–2005 **12305-0D022**, and a generic catalog replacement **12305-0D023**. The *factory bulletin* supplies an additional **12305-0D120** engineering revision, but does **not** establish that 0D120 can replace a June-2003 0D021-equipped Canadian-built Corolla. Nor does a catalog 0D023 supersession automatically prove the same redesign as the bulletin 0D120.

**Research gate:** VIN-specific Toyota EPC compatibility and factory engineering notice; compare mounting insulator shape, through-bolt/bushing, brackets, installed height, engine/transmission application and production plant. Until verified, **DO NOT recommend/install 12305-0D120** on the subject Corolla. If this car has drive-off drone, test mounts and alternative vibration sources before replacing anything.
