# Upgrade recommendation criteria — benefits and installation first

Decision recorded 2026-10-25: owner prioritizes **actual benefits and installation requirements** over simple product lists. This preference is research-method guidance, not a maintenance or corrosion assessment.

For every shortlisted upgrade, report in this order:

1. **Benefits** — precisely what improves (power/torque, handling response, ride quality, durability, comfort, efficiency); distinguish measured independent results, manufacturer tests/claims, engineering inference, and restoration of worn baseline. No invented gains.
2. **Installation requirements** — tools, whether vehicle lifting is required, disassembly boundaries, special procedures, safety-dependent steps, supporting hardware, fluids, wiring/ECU calibration, and post-install verification. Clearly mark unknown torque/procedure details.
3. **DIY feasibility** — basic / intermediate / advanced / specialist, and specific skills required; never classify complex transmission or SRS work as trivial.
4. **Compatibility gates** — June 2003 production, A245E, current aftermarket intake/18-inch wheel constraints as applicable; candidate/probable/confirmed status.
5. **Downsides** — adverse effects on ride, sound, durability, braking/handling balance, emissions or road legality.
6. **Cost** — dated product and complete-install economics when sourced, separate from benefits.

Recommendation tiers:
- **Actionable conditional:** strong manufacturer application documentation, known installation method, plausible benefit, bounded remaining checks.
- **Research-only:** major fitment or installation/control/legality gaps.
- **Not supported:** no credible benefit evidence or demonstrably wrong application.

- **Eibach 8271.140 springs:** nominal manufacturer lowering and body-control change. Four corners require safe coil-spring handling, strut disassembly/assembly and alignment; 18-inch wheel clearance not yet verified.
- **KYB Excel-G struts:** damping restoration if originals are worn; full corner-by-corner SKU and upper mounts need catalog check; strut service, spring-handling and alignment.
- **FRAM XG4967 filter:** manufacturer filtration construction; routine oil-filter installation. No vehicle-specific durability improvement proven.
- **Toyota revised 17171-22060 intake gasket:** addresses documented cold-weather seal-related lean condition; intake removal and factory torque/leak procedure; exact June-build applicability pending.
- **TRD 1ZZ supercharger:** potential substantial power; specialized mechanical, fueling and matched TRD ECU installation; legacy package current legality and parts completeness unknown.
- **TransGo SK A246:** hydraulic/shift-specific valve-body kit; advanced precision transmission service, model-specific EPC confirmation and instruction verification necessary.

Research should continue independently without waiting on physical inspection. Keep condition assessments out of scope unless requested.

## Mandatory healthy-OEM baseline and value comparison

**Owner decision (2026-10-25):** Compare each proposed replacement against a **new or properly functioning factory-specification equivalent**, not a worn, defective, or neglected original. Report the incremental benefit of paying for the upgrade, not the gains from restoring baseline function.

For each candidate, capture:

1. **Healthy baseline:** identify the OE part/system and how it performs when functioning to factory specification. If data are missing, write `unknown` rather than inventing an OEM performance number.
2. **Like-for-like conditions:** vehicle, tire and wheel package, engine/transmission calibration, road/track/temperature, test method and relevant maintenance condition held as constant as practical. Note any uncontrolled variables.
3. **Incremental gains:** report only `upgraded − healthy OEM`; identify source and test method. Separate measurable power, torque, braking distance/fade, body roll, damping, service life, noise, efficiency and subjective feel. Manufacturer estimates and owner anecdotes are not independent measurements.
4. **Incremental cost:** upgrade installed total minus the total cost of fitting a **healthy standard replacement** when replacement is needed. For an optional modification to a healthy car, compare the full upgrade cost against **keeping the healthy OE system ($0 intervention)**. Include required supporting parts, tools, specialist labor, taxes/fees where sourced and post-install alignment/tuning as applicable. Unknown costs remain unknown.
5. **Install comparison:** extra labor and tooling *over stock replacement*, complexity and risk, plus downtime and reversible/irreversible changes.
6. **Tradeoffs and verdict:** document comfort/noise/handling balance/legal/warranty/reliability consequences. Rate `demonstrated improvement`, `plausible but unmeasured`, `restoration only`, or `unsubstantiated/negative` separately from fitment confidence. Only call a product **worth the premium** when evidence of the incremental benefit reasonably supports its additional cost for the stated goal.

A new KYB or OE strut making a worn original ride better is **restoration**, not proof it outperforms a healthy original. A premium oil filter's media specification is not proof of reduced engine wear over a quality OEM filter. An intake manifold gasket revision aimed at a known leak may improve failure resistance but not add horsepower to an airtight healthy baseline. A performance spring or sway bar may have an observable geometry/response difference from factory even when OEM parts are healthy, but any quantified handling improvement needs comparably controlled measurements and comfort/oversteer tradeoffs.

Apply this rule retroactively to the existing 148 research entries before publishing buying recommendations. Do not confuse **fitment verified** with **benefit demonstrated**.

## Performance is a multidimensional outcome — owner clarification 2026-10-25

**Do not equate performance with horsepower.** Compare modifications against a *healthy factory* system across these distinct outcomes: (1) electrical efficiency — watts, amperes, on-time, alternator loading; (2) mechanical efficiency — parasitic/frictional and rolling losses; (3) thermal behavior — heat rejection, intake charge, component temperature; (4) dynamic performance — tire grip, braking fade, steering/roll, response; (5) durability — service life and failure margin; (6) quality/capability — usable light, air filtration, quietness, diagnostic visibility. Report all relevant categories even if peak power remains unchanged.

**Rank electrical reductions by actual watts saved × duty cycle and circuit safety**, not just claimed LED brightness or novelty. Distinguish measured electrical reduction from estimated alternator load and fuel savings; *never* promise a meaningful mpg gain from an intermittent 5W bulb. Include total load of anti-hyperflash resistors/adapters and reject conversions that compromise legal lighting. Prefer manufacturer current draw, at-voltage test, and proper optical application. Low-cost/easy constraints still apply. See [Electrical efficiency audit](../research/LOW_COST_ELECTRICAL_EFFICIENCY.md).

## Fuel economy and net system efficiency — owner requirement (2026-10-25)

Fuel saving and whole-car efficiency are **primary performance objectives**, not an afterthought to horsepower. Evaluate **MPG/gallons per 100 miles, annual gallons and dollars saved, net electrical load (watts × hours), charging/alternator demand, rolling resistance, friction, accessory parasitic losses, and thermal efficiency**. Use real measured baseline/upgrade results wherever possible and explicit ranges/assumptions otherwise. 

Require **equal system functionality and legal/safe operation** when comparing: lighting must retain proper beam and signaling; tires must retain suitable grip; engine/cooling controls must operate appropriately. Higher efficiency in one component may impose extra load or risk elsewhere: hyperflash resistors, added cooling pumps/fans, filter pressure drop, sidewall and tread tradeoffs, added electronics standby draw. Sum system-level energy use instead of counting isolated marketing wattage reductions.

For every recommendation report the **healthy OEM baseline, updated net electrical/mechanical/thermal input, duty cycle, estimated or measured MPG/gallons/year, cost/year and payback, install burden, source quality and constraints**. Separate measured fuel gain from modeled estimates and distinguish savings versus current installed modified equipment from savings versus factory baseline. See [fuel and system-efficiency framework](../research/FUEL_AND_SYSTEM_EFFICIENCY_FRAMEWORK.md).
