# Felicity — body sealing, wind noise, and transmission-line NVH bulletins (2026-10-25)

**Vehicle**: owner-described US-market 2004 Corolla LE, Canada-built June 2003, 1ZZ-FE/A245E. **Evidence type:** Toyota TSB text reproduced in service-manual archives, plus third-party automotive references. No symptom, original part fit, repair or tested noise reduction has been observed on Felicity. Vehicle-specific VIN and original bulletin diagrams/part tables are outstanding.

## 1. Strong factory corrective-engineering evidence: door glass runs

**Toyota NV006-05 (revised April 19, 2005; later revision notice July 19, 2006):** “Wind Noise From Door Glass Window Run,” **2003–2005 Corolla North American Production**. Toyota states the **window run was improved** to remedy customer-reported front or rear door-glass wind noise. Replacement process includes removing original tape, thorough frame cleaning, correct run positioning, and adhering the upper portion with a plastic tool. Do not lubricate glass run during insertion according to TSB.

Source: https://charm.li/Toyota/2004/Corolla%20L4-1.8L%20%281ZZ-FE%29/Repair%20and%20Diagnosis/Technical%20Service%20Bulletins/Customer%20Interest/Body%20-%20Front%2FRear%20Door%20Glass%20Wind%20Noises/

**Critical fitment:** bulletin distinguishes before/after VIN thresholds with **different part numbers**. Full illustrated table and original manufacturer parts must be captured before shopping. Because the car was Canadian-built, confirm the specific NAP/TMMC plant designation through factory EPC, not the general term “North America.” No generic adhesive weatherstrip stuffing approved.

**Upgrade classification:** Factory-documented redesign to address a particular healthy-as-built wind-noise issue on affected models. Symptom-driven modernizing repair; **not a proven quieter-than-healthy-revised-OEM add-on**.

## 2. Rear door weatherstrip retention

**Toyota BO020-04**, November 30, 2004, **2003–2005 Corolla TMMC** before specified production effective VIN, describes a loose rear door opening weatherstrip near the C-pillar. Correction adds **two clips** to quarter-panel flange and reinstalls the original seal.

Source: https://charm.li/Toyota/2004/Corolla%20L4-1.8L%20%281ZZ-FE%29/Repair%20and%20Diagnosis/Technical%20Service%20Bulletins/Customer%20Interest/Rear%20Door%20Weatherstrip%20Loose/

**Potential relevance:** Toyota Motor Manufacturing Canada is more specific than a generic catalog model year and materially relevant to Canada-built car, but individual VIN cutoff still requires confirmation. Don't assume looseness or buy generic trim clips. Avoid sealing door drain paths.

## 3. Windshield 'reed vibration' moldings

**Toyota T-SB-0257-08**, August 26, 2008 (supersedes NV004-07), describes a **buzz or reed-like sound** as passing air lifts and releases windshield rubber molding. Toyota lists a diagnosis and body repair procedure. **US 2003–2008 Corolla applicability must be confirmed from the complete bulletin image/table**; a manufacturer reproduction appears in a 2005 service-manual section and identifies 2003–08 Corolla family.

Sources: https://charm.li/Toyota/2005/Corolla%20L4-1.8L%20%282ZZ-GE%29/Repair%20and%20Diagnosis/Body%20and%20Frame/Exterior%20Moulding%20%2F%20Trim/Technical%20Service%20Bulletins/Customer%20Interest/Body%20-%20Windshield%20Wind%20Noise/ ; Canadian **TSB2729** dated March 26, 2007 for 2003–2008 Corolla: https://charm.li/Toyota/2005/Corolla%20L4-1.8L%20%281ZZ-FE%29/Repair%20and%20Diagnosis/Technical%20Service%20Bulletins/Customer%20Interest/Canada%20-%20Body%20-%20Windshield%20Wind%20Noise/

**Do not improvise** trim glue, household caulk or body drilling. Identify whether noise truly comes from molding rather than door runs, glass alignment or mirror. Replacement of structural windshield adhesive or disturbed glazing is a professional job.

## 4. Important crossover: A245E transmission noise from cooler lines

**Toyota TC012-05** (revised; Sept 1 2005; updated 2007), **2003–2008 Corolla with 1ZZ-FE and automatic**, describes whistle/hoot during light throttle at **35–40 mph (56–64 km/h)** at normal operating transmission temperature. Toyota redesigned **automatic transmission oil cooler lines** to address this specific NVH issue. Installation guidance warns against bent/twisted lines, contamination, and requires fluid-level/leak check after work.

US source: https://charm.li/Toyota/2007/Corolla%20L4-1.8L%20%281ZZ-FE%29/Repair%20and%20Diagnosis/Transmission%20and%20Drivetrain/Technical%20Service%20Bulletins/Customer%20Interest/A%2FT%20-%20Whistle%2FHoot%20Noise%20at%2035-40%20MPH/

Canadian **TSB2306R** Sept 15, 2005 (revised 2007), likewise lists 2003–2008 Corolla 1ZZ-FE automatic: https://charm.li/Toyota/2003/Corolla%20CE%20Sedan%20L4-1.8L%20%281ZZ-FE%29/Repair%20and%20Diagnosis/Technical%20Service%20Bulletins/Customer%20Interest/Canada%20-%20A%2FT%20-%20Whistle%2FHoot%20Noise%20on%20Light%20Acceleration/

This adds a **genuine Corolla-specific engineered NVH correction** to earlier work on transmission thermal management. It is NOT evidence that the transmission requires an extra cooler, a new solenoid or a rebuild. The full TSB's parts table/diagram and VIN-derived original cooler-line configuration must be verified before recommending a particular line or disassembly.

## 5. Aero: baseline and measurement limits

Edmunds generic 2004 Corolla sedan specification lists **Cd 0.30**. This is a model-family reference, not a certified Cd for owner's exact aero hardware. A transport-research appendix quotes **2004 Corolla SE Cd 0.31 and frontal area 1.90 m²**, a variant discrepancy that reinforces **not** using a single unverified drag area to compute fuel savings.

Sources: https://www.edmunds.com/toyota/corolla/2004/sedan/features-specs/ ; https://onlinepubs.trb.org/onlinepubs/nchrp/nchrp_rpt_720Appendixes.pdf (appendix A, p.A-22).

Decisions:
- Refit missing **stock** undertray/splash guards, wheel liner, grille hardware or trim if absent/damaged as **restoration**, not aero gains beyond an intact healthy factory car.
- **No** homebuilt grille blocking in an uninstrumented daily driver: risks overheating engine/transmission and A/C performance; no measured pressure or aero benefit.
- **No** fabricated spoilers, mirror deletion, lowered suspension, unvalidated smooth wheel covers or loose underbody panels. Potential detachment, legal/visibility, brake/engine heat and poor ROI.
- No fuel-saving percentages attributed to weatherstrip work. Better seals may reduce wind whistle/drafts but lower CdA requires controlled wind-tunnel/coastdown evidence beyond subjective noise.
